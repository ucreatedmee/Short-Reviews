You Are Not Your Craft: Separating Self-Worth from Output
In her recent interview, Eileen Gu stated that "you are not your craft," a concept which suggests that people should focus on usable feedback instead of wasting energy to protect their own pride.
I argue that individuals should not equate their personal identity with their work, because failing to distinguish the two prevents people from accepting criticism objectively.
To begin with, people tend to protect themselves from criticism. For instance, when students receive negative feedback on their essays, they often react defensively and make vague excuses to cover up mistakes, which prevents them from improving. This is because defending one’s pride leaves no room for improvement. Blindly refusing to accept feedback blocks out a vital way of improving oneself.
Admittedly, some might argue that separating oneself from one's craft will lead to a lack of passion. However, I would respond that individuals should implement usable feedback instead of spending the energy to fix damaged self esteem. Focusing on the right feedback can lead to a better understanding of oneself which ends up with more passion to overcome difficulties.
To sum up, individuals who can distinguish between self worth and  output will be more likely to achieve sustained growth. Gu’s statement highlights that an individual’s self worth should not be tied up to their current performance. By treating criticism as data rather than personal attacks, individuals can improve their skills while maintaining emotional stability. This is why separating self-worth from output is essential for individuals to achieve true growth.
